Background

Before we dig in, what is MM?

Marketing messages (MM) are a message-based ad format for messaging opted-in customers on WhatsApp, Instagram, or Messenger. This project focused on WhatsApp. A WhatsApp MM lands straight in the customer's inbox, and a reply opens a real conversation, unlike a standard ad. Advertisers use MM to reach people who have already opted in, so it carries a level of trust and directness that standard ads don't, and that's exactly what makes the reply, and everything that happens after it, worth designing for.

Problem

Buried, Then Silent

MM setup was buried in a separate modal experience under Creative, where advertisers configured text, Auto Reply, and Quick Replies together in one area, apart from the ad itself. As a result, most advertisers never finished setting it up, and the marketing message was never delivered at all.

Marketing messages are one-directional today. 60% of consumers read them once delivered, but only ~1% end up in a real conversation. Once a consumer messages back, the conversation just stops.

Background

The platform powering Walmart's third-party sellers

Walmart Seller Center had no dedicated homepage. Sellers landed directly on the items page with no sense of context, priority, or what to do next. I led the end-to-end design of the first-ever homepage from scratch, from competitive analysis through to launch and design QA.

The Problem

There was no homepage. Sellers had nowhere to start

Without a dedicated landing page, sellers went straight to the items page. Unrelated notifications appeared on this page to alert sellers, with no sense of priority or context. There was no homepage that could help sellers understand their business, complete tasks, or grow their store.

Defining what belongs on the homepage

I ran a content workshop with the product team to determine the essential requirements across three core areas: key business metrics, tasks, and growth. Usability Testing

Testing comprehension: new design vs. original onboarding

We tested this first-round design using the PURE (Practical Usability Rating by Experts) method with 5 evaluators. To set a baseline, we first tested the original onboarding flow, then integrated that onboarding into the new homepage to see how the To-do section improved seller comprehension.

Original onboarding flow

The original onboarding prioritized the "Add Items" banner over the actual setup steps. Sellers were pushed toward listing products before completing essential account setup, causing confusion and drop-off.

New onboarding flow

By integrating onboarding into the To-do section, sellers could clearly see what setup steps remained, in the right order, with the right priority, without being distracted by promotions.

Background

So, what's a partnership ad?

Partnership ad allow advertisers to run ads with partners: creators, brands, and other businesses. The advertiser and partner accounts are featured in the ad's header and the ads leverage signals from both accounts for improved ranking and incremental performance.

Dual header (Brand+Creator)
Single header (Brand only)
Single header (Creator only)
Problem

The One Gap Left

Right now, enabling a partnership ad during campaign creation means the Marketing Message (MM) simply doesn't send.

Our main objective is to unblock that 5% of eligible revenue, by enabling partnership ads for MM and bringing them to full feature parity with the rest of the ad creative experience for WhatsApp MM.

Consumer Design

Showing the Partnership

On the consumer side, we had to think through how a partnership ad would actually look inside an MM. Instagram and Facebook posts have their own dedicated header for surfacing a partner, but an MM lives directly inside a chat thread with the consumer, so we needed a different way to represent the partnership there.

Exploration

We ran several rounds of review with the WhatsApp, Partnership Ad, and MM XFN teams across a set of tag placement directions before settling on an approach.

Tag burned into the photo
Tag as a layer outside the media

The direction we landed on had to meet a few requirements:

✓
Stays separate from the media a partner or advertiser actually submits, never composited into the photo or video pixels
✓
Doesn't read as tappable or overly Instagram branded, since policy doesn't allow deep linking out to Instagram from the ad
✓
Flattens into a single delivered image via ACO, rather than staying a separate live UI element

Three Options, XFN Reviewed

Once we landed on where the tag would sit, the next question was how much of the partner's identity to surface. We considered the following directions with the WhatsApp team before landing on the one that shipped.

✓ Option 1
Creator's profile photo alone
Option 2
Photo with a small Instagram badge
Option 3
Instagram logo itself

We proposed Option 1, it won for two reasons:

✓
Doesn't look tappable, so people won't mistake it for a link, consumers are only ever redirected to the destination URL either way
✓
Says less about IG/FB's involvement than the other options, a fine tradeoff since the business is Meta Verified

Final Consumer Design

To display partnership ads in MM, we use image burn-in automated creative optimization (ACO) to embed the partnership tag directly within the media asset.

  • Appears as a long label beneath the media
  • Includes the partner's profile image, username, and the word "Partnership"

What about long usernames?

The maximum username length is 30 characters for Instagram and 50 characters for Facebook. If a username exceeds the available space for the partnership ad tag due to media width constraints, it's truncated to fit within the media width. This keeps the tag visually consistent and prevents it from overflowing or disrupting the layout.

ACO was intentionally a fast first pass, not the end state. If it performs well, the plan is to go back and build a full, proper design with the WhatsApp team.
